# project-cafe-commit-roles

Here's a set of roles in the same register as the script. The core three keep a meeting running, and the rest support the group over time.

**Core roles (every meeting)**

**Facilitator.** Runs the meeting from the script, keeps it moving, and makes the transitions. During blocks, they hold the questions-before-suggestions rule. During crit, they make sure feedback stays about the work and that one voice doesn't dominate. They also invite quieter people in when it's natural.

**Timekeeper.** Keeps rounds and turns to their limits and gives a gentle signal when someone is running over. This sounds minor, but it's what keeps things fair, and it spares the facilitator from being the one who always cuts people off.

**Host.** Welcomes newcomers, explains how things work before the meeting starts, and checks in with them afterward. Online, this person also handles the practical side: links, breakout rooms, screen-sharing permissions.

**Ongoing roles (rotate less often)**

**Crit lead (optional).** If the group grows, it can help to have a separate person run the crit half, since pushing feedback toward specifics takes different skills than holding space for blocks. Small groups can leave this with the facilitator.

**Notes keeper.** Keeps a light shared record: who brought what, anything worth following up on, decisions made. Not minutes, just continuity, so next time someone can ask, "Did you get that deploy working?"

**Conduct contacts (two people).** The people members go to if something goes wrong. Having two means there's always an option if the concern involves one of them. They should rotate less often than the others, maybe every six months, because trust takes time to build.

**Rotation**

The meeting roles work well rotating monthly, or every three to four sessions. That's long enough to get comfortable in a role and short enough that nobody becomes permanent. A simple rule helps: nobody holds the same role twice in a row, and new members shadow a role once before taking it on. Keep a sign-up list in the notes so the closing round only needs a quick "who's got next month?"

A small group can combine roles. The facilitator can also keep time, and the host can take notes. The one role I'd keep separate is conduct contacts, so they're not also the people running meetings.
