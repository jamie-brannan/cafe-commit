# project-cafe-commit-cycle

Hopefully if we get going, we'll have a virtuous circle

## After the meeting (same day)

Host: sends a short direct message to any newcomers: thanks for coming, any questions, see you next time. This is the single biggest factor in whether newcomers come back.

Notes keeper: posts a brief recap in the async channel within a day. It should cover which pieces of work were critiqued, any follow-ups people committed to, and the carryover queue (anyone who didn't get time and goes first next session). Blocks stay out of the notes unless the person explicitly wants a follow-up thread. That keeps the blocks half candid.

##  During the week

Presenters: post an update in their crit thread if they tried something: "went with the second approach, here's how it turned out." This closes the loop for reviewers and shows the feedback mattered.

Everyone: blocks follow-ups happen only if the person who shared opens the conversation. Others can offer ("happy to pair on that if useful") but don't chase.

##  3–4 days before the next meeting

Host: posts the call for intentions: attending, bringing a block, bringing crit work, or listening. Carryover people are confirmed first.

Crit presenters: post a link (gist, repo, sandbox, design file) plus three things: brief context, where they're stuck or unsure, and what kind of feedback they want. That last part matters more than it seems. "Is this architecture sound?" and "does this naming read clearly?" need very different reviews, and asking up front saves half the crit time.

Reviewers: optionally skim the posted work ahead of time. Not required, but it makes crit sharper.

##  1 day before
Facilitator: closes sign-ups, runs the time math with N, B, and C, and posts the agenda with times and the format (plenary or breakout rooms).

Host: sets up the breakout room plan if needed, checks Zoom settings (waiting room, recording off, screen-share permissions), and sends the link privately to members.

##  Day of, 15 minutes before

Host: opens the room and admits people from the waiting room. Gives newcomers a quick pre-brief on how things work.
Facilitator and timekeeper: quick sync on the agenda and anything unusual, like a bigger turnout than expected or a late crit submission.

##  Monthly
At the end of the last meeting each month, or in a short separate session, hold a retro: what's working, what isn't, and any proposed format changes. Decisions happen here, not mid-meeting. Role rotation sign-ups for the next month happen at the same time. Conduct contacts check in privately with each other about anything they've seen.
Once the pieces are settled, I can assemble everything so far (purpose statement, script, roles, time math, and this cycle) into a single doc your group can share and edit.
